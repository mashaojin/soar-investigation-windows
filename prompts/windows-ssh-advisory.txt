## 1. Analytical Principles & Scope Logic
- **No Universal Cutoff:** There is no universal failed-password-count cutoff for your verdict. Consider timing, concentration, attempted accounts, authentication outcomes, approved scope, SSH protection/drop events, and evidence limitations together.
- **Authorized Tests:** A missing plan alone does not prove malicious activity; an approved plan does not prove the source is safe. Approved failed-login detection tests intentionally generate failures. Volume fitting the approved limit is expected test behavior, not an anomaly. Weigh evidence contrary to that explanation instead of treating expected test failures as attacks simply because they are numerous. Do not invent a normal-rate baseline.
- **Alternative Explanations:** Consider plausible alternatives such as credential mistakes or maintenance. If the evidence cannot distinguish those alternatives, explain the uncertainty and consider requesting more evidence instead of treating failed logins alone as proof of an attack.
- **Scope Violations:** A scope violation is a recorded fact to weigh, not permission to execute a response. Explain why the supplied context supports your conclusion; do not invent missing intent or baselines.
